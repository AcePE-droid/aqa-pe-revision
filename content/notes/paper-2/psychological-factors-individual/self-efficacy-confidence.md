## Confidence: Trait, State and the Vealey Model

**Confidence** is a belief in the ability to master a task, and is closely linked to approach behaviour – confident performers are more likely to take risks and welcome challenge.

- **Trait confidence** is a consistent level of confidence shown across most situations, largely unaffected by pressure or anxiety
- **State confidence** is confidence specific to a particular situation, which can vary depending on the demands of that situation and the performer's prior experience of it
- The **Vealey model of sports confidence** explains how trait confidence and competitive orientation combine within a specific **objective sporting situation** to produce **state confidence**, which then affects performance. The outcome is subjectively judged by the performer, and this judgement feeds back to shape future trait confidence and competitive orientation

## Bandura's Self-Efficacy Theory and Improving Confidence

**Self-efficacy** is a belief in the ability to master a specific sporting situation – essentially, task-specific confidence, as described by Bandura (1977).

- Bandura identified four factors affecting self-efficacy: **performance accomplishments** (past success in a task increases confidence for future attempts); **vicarious experience** (watching a similar model succeed at a task can increase the observer's own confidence, especially if the model is perceived as similar in ability); **verbal persuasion** (encouragement from significant others such as coaches or respected teammates reinforces belief in the ability to succeed); and **emotional arousal** (how a performer interprets their physiological arousal – interpreting it positively supports confidence, interpreting it negatively as anxiety undermines it)
- Coaches and players can use these four factors to improve confidence – e.g. controlling arousal through relaxation, giving accurate demonstrations, pointing out past successful performances, offering encouragement, setting attainable goals, and using mental rehearsal
- The **home field advantage** links to self-efficacy – playing in a familiar environment in front of a supportive crowd tends to increase confidence and assertive behaviour, though very large or hostile crowds can sometimes increase anxiety and cause performance to suffer instead
