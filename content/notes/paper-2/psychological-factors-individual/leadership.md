## Leadership: Types and Qualities

A **leader** is someone who influences others towards achieving goals, playing a key role in maintaining motivation and setting team targets.

- Leaders can be classified by how they are appointed – a **prescribed leader** is appointed from outside the group (e.g. by a higher authority), often bringing new ideas; an **emergent leader** is appointed from within the existing group, often preserving established methods and group harmony
- Effective leaders typically show qualities such as **charisma**, strong **communication**, **interpersonal skills**, **empathy**, relevant **experience**, an **inspirational** manner, **confidence**, and good **organisational skills**

## Styles of Leadership (Lewin's Three Styles)

Lewin (1939) identified three styles of leadership, with the most effective style depending on the situation:

- **Autocratic (task-oriented)** – the leader makes all decisions and dictates instructions to the group; best suited to situations requiring quick decisions and clear results
- **Democratic (person-oriented)** – the leader consults the group and seeks opinions before making decisions; supports interpersonal relationships and group cohesion, but takes longer to reach decisions
- **Laissez-faire** – the leader leaves most decisions to the group and takes a hands-off role; can work well with highly experienced, self-sufficient groups, but risks reduced motivation and poor discipline without guidance

## Fiedler's Contingency Model of Leadership

**Fiedler's contingency model** suggests the most effective leadership style depends on how favourable the situation is for the leader – based on factors such as the leader's respect and position of authority, group ability and motivation, task clarity, and the leader-group relationship.

- A **task-oriented (autocratic)** style is most effective in both **very favourable** situations (e.g. high respect, good relationships, clear task, high motivation) and **very unfavourable** situations (e.g. low respect, poor relationships, low motivation, unclear task)
- A **person-oriented (democratic)** style is most effective in **moderately favourable** situations, where building interpersonal relationships and consulting the group is more valuable

## Chelladurai's Multi-Dimensional Model of Leadership

Chelladurai (1980) argued that leadership effectiveness depends on more than just the situation – it also depends on matching leadership style to the requirements of the situation, the needs of the group, and the leader's own preferred style.

- The model considers three types of leader behaviour: **required behaviour** (what the situation demands), **actual behaviour** (what the leader decides to do), and **preferred behaviour** (what the group wants)
- The situation is shaped by characteristics of the task, the leader, and the group (e.g. group size, gender, age, experience) – e.g. a large, dangerous or time-pressured situation may require a more autocratic, instructive approach, while an experienced, low-danger situation may suit a more democratic approach
- The closer the match between required, actual and preferred behaviour, the higher the resulting group performance and satisfaction
